LLM inferenceisan operating system

Every hard problem in serving a language model is a problem someone already solved for a 1970s time-sharing kernel — and then someone re-derived it, badly, in Python. These problems start from a real serving scenario and escalate until the candidate is standing in front of paging, preemption, deadlock, cache coherence, speculative execution, and barrier synchronisation without anyone having said those words out loud.

How to run it

Read the setup aloud, then hand over one stage at a time. Never show the next stage early — the escalation is the signal.

Timebox

One problem per 45-minute slot. Most candidates reach stage 3. Stage 5+ is where you separate the top few percent.

Scoring

Every stage lists three tiers of answer. Grade the reasoning path, not the landing spot — a wrong answer with the right invariant beats a memorised one.

If they stall

Use the nudge at the bottom of each solution. Giving a hint costs nothing; watching someone flail costs you the signal.

Numbers assume an A100-80GB (≈312 TFLOP/s bf16 dense, ≈2.0 TB/s HBM) unless stated. They're there so candidates have to compute rather than gesture; swap in your own hardware and the shape of every answer holds.
