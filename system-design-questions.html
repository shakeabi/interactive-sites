Senior / staff interview drills · five problems, twenty-five rungs

Every design is fine until the next order of magnitude.

Interviewers at this level don't score your first diagram. They score how you find its breaking point, name the cost of fixing it, and keep going. So these problems are built as ladders: a working design, the load that kills it, the repair, and the new failure the repair created.

Predict, then read

Each rung ends with the failure that's already baked in. Call it before you open the answer — that guess is the skill being tested.

Watch the readouts

Chips show what the current design actually delivers. When one turns red, that's the rung's reason to exist.

Play the widgets

Four live models — burst math, capacity, fanout cost, crash outcomes. Move the inputs until the numbers stop feeling abstract.

Say the trade out loud

Every fix here costs something. The candidates who pass are the ones who volunteer the cost before being asked.

    What the scorecard says

    • Scoping. Did you cut the problem down before designing, and name what you're deliberately not building?
    • Numbers. Did the estimate change a decision, or was it decoration?
    • Bottleneck instinct. Given your own design, can you find the next thing to break without being led there?
    • Trade articulation. Every choice named with its cost, in latency, dollars, staleness, or operational pain.
    • Failure thinking. What happens on crash, partition, retry storm, or bad deploy — volunteered, not extracted.
    • Depth on demand. One component you can go three levels deep on when asked.
