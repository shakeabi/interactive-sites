Part I strips the system down to something you can hold in your head, at three levels of zoom. Part II takes the twelve decisions that shaped it and lays out the full option space for each — what was chosen, what was written down and rejected, and what was never on the table at all. That last category is the interesting one: the gap between a team's documented alternatives and the real menu is usually where the unexamined assumptions live.

Part I

The system, simplified

Three zoom levels. Each is complete at its own resolution — start at the top and stop wherever you have enough.

The two middle boxes are the entire product. Anyone can write the first, fourth and fifth. Deciding whether a request may spend the firm's money right now, and which of many interchangeable-but-separately-metered provider deployments should serve it — that is the system.
Accounting happens twice, on two clocks. Redis is updated synchronously because the very next request needs to see it — that is what makes quotas work. The durable billing row is published to Kafka and written by a separate process, because nothing on the request path should wait for a database. ADMIT and ROUTE are the two stages with real algorithms in them; everything else is plumbing.
Three things this view makes visible. The dashed loop is the only path that survives a Kafka outage — spool to NFS, replay back into Kafka so there is still exactly one way data reaches the store. The red annotation is the system's largest standing bet: when Redis is unreachable every gate reads zero and opens. And the control plane sits on its own pool, so saturating inference does not stop you listing your projects.
