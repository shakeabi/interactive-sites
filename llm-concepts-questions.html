Plausible≠Correct

Almost every question here has an answer that sounds informed and is wrong — and the wrong answer is usually the one that's been repeated most often online. So each stage opens by naming the answer you'll probably get, next to what's actually true. If a candidate walks straight past the trap, that's your signal. If they walk into it and can be pulled out, that's a different and still useful signal.

How to run it

Pick one track per interview slot. Hand over one stage at a time; the escalation is the measurement.

Don't read the trap aloud

The amber box is for you. Telling the candidate the common wrong answer hands them the question.

Scoring

Three tiers per stage. Grade the reasoning, not the vocabulary — fluency with jargon is the single most misleading signal in this field.

Interns especially

Nobody clears every track. Depth in one plus honest "I don't know" elsewhere beats shallow coverage of all five.
